Cucumbers (Cucumis sativus) are one of the most refreshing and widely cultivated members of the gourd family. Though often treated as a vegetable in the culinary world, they are botanically classified as a fruit because they develop from flowers and contain seeds. With a water content of approximately 95%, they are a staple for hydration and a favorite in global cuisines.

Cucumbers thrive in warm climates and require consistent moisture to flourish. They are typically grown as annual plants and can be trellised or left to sprawl along the ground. In regions with shorter growing seasons, they are often started indoors and transplanted as seedlings. Proper spacing is essential for air circulation, which helps prevent fungal diseases. Harvesting is usually done when the cucumbers are firm and green, typically 50-70 days after planting, depending on the variety.
